Consent & Privacy Guide

Understand the fundamentals of privacy and user consent in digital advertising, including GDPR, CCPA, consent management platforms, consent strings, cookies and privacy-related AdTech workflows.

What Is Privacy in Digital Advertising?

Privacy in digital advertising refers to how websites, applications and advertising technologies collect, use, share and protect information relating to users.

Modern advertising ecosystems may involve publishers, advertisers, DSPs, SSPs, ad exchanges, analytics platforms, identity providers and other technology vendors.

👤

User Data

Information that may be associated with a user, browser, device or online activity.

🍪

Cookies & Storage

Browser technologies used for functionality, measurement, personalization and advertising.

🔐

Consent

User choices that can determine whether certain data processing or advertising activities occur.

GDPR & Digital Advertising

The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) is a European Union privacy regulation that governs the processing of personal data in situations covered by the regulation.

Why GDPR Matters to Publishers

Publishers serving users in applicable jurisdictions may need to consider how personal data is collected and used for analytics, personalization, advertising and other purposes.

Common AdTech Considerations

  • Providing appropriate privacy information to users.
  • Obtaining consent where required.
  • Respecting user choices.
  • Managing third-party vendors and purposes.
  • Passing relevant consent information to advertising technology partners.
Important: Privacy requirements depend on factors such as the user's location, applicable laws, processing activities and the organization's role. This guide is educational and is not legal advice.

CCPA & CPRA

The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA), as amended by the California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA), provides privacy rights to eligible California residents.

Advertising Considerations

  • Understand applicable consumer privacy rights.
  • Provide required privacy disclosures.
  • Handle applicable opt-out requests.
  • Consider how data is shared with advertising partners.
  • Ensure applicable signals and user choices are respected.

CCPA/CPRA requirements are different from GDPR. A publisher should not assume that implementing one privacy framework automatically satisfies another.

What Is a Consent Management Platform?

A Consent Management Platform (CMP) helps websites and applications present privacy choices to users and manage the resulting consent or preference signals.

📋

Consent Collection

Presents users with information and choices relating to applicable processing activities.

🔄

Consent Signals

Helps communicate user choices to participating advertising and technology platforms.

⚙️

Vendor Management

Can help publishers manage purposes, vendors and privacy-related configurations.

Typical CMP Flow

1 User Visits Site
2 Privacy Notice
3 User Choice
4 Consent Signal
5 Ad Request

Consent Strings in AdTech

Consent strings are machine-readable signals used to communicate privacy and consent choices between components of an advertising ecosystem.

Why Consent Strings Matter

An ad request may pass privacy-related information to downstream advertising partners. Those partners can use the applicable signals to determine how they should process the request.

Common Concepts

  • User consent
  • Legitimate interest
  • Vendor permissions
  • Advertising purposes
  • Measurement purposes
  • Privacy and jurisdiction signals
AdTech tip: When troubleshooting monetization problems, privacy signals should be considered alongside ad requests, demand sources, consent configuration and geographic traffic.

Cookies & Browser Storage

Cookies and other browser storage technologies can be used for different purposes. Not every cookie has the same privacy implications or legal requirements.

Type Common Purpose Advertising Relevance
Essential Website functionality Usually focused on site operation
Analytics Measurement and performance Can provide traffic and engagement data
Advertising Advertising and personalization Directly relevant to ad monetization
Preference Remembering user settings Depends on implementation

Privacy in the Advertising Request Flow

Privacy information can influence how an advertising request is processed. A simplified publisher advertising flow can look like this:

1 User
2 Publisher Site
3 CMP
4 Ad Request
5 Demand Partners

If a user does not provide a particular permission, the publisher and its technology partners may need to adjust how the request is handled depending on the applicable privacy framework and configuration.

Publisher Privacy Checklist

Use this checklist as a starting point when reviewing privacy and consent implementation.

✅ Privacy policy is available and appropriate for the website or application.
✅ Applicable privacy requirements have been identified for target audiences and jurisdictions.
✅ CMP configuration has been reviewed.
✅ Consent and privacy signals are being passed correctly where required.
✅ Advertising partners and vendors are reviewed periodically.
✅ Privacy-related changes are tested before production deployment.

Common AdTech Privacy Problems

⚠️

Low Fill Rate

Incorrect or missing privacy signals can sometimes affect demand eligibility and monetization.

🔍

Missing Consent Signal

Inspect the consent-management implementation and advertising request to determine whether relevant signals are being transmitted.

🧪

Testing Issues

Test different geographic locations, consent choices and browser environments when troubleshooting.
