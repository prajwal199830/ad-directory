SSP, Ad Server, Ad Network & Ad Exchange

Let's break down some of the most commonly confused components of the digital advertising ecosystem.

EXPLORE Ad Server Ad Network SSP Ad Exchange Ecosystem
ADTECH FUNDAMENTALS

Why Do These Platforms Exist?

Digital advertising involves multiple technologies that help publishers manage inventory and help advertisers find opportunities to reach audiences.

Terms such as SSP, ad server, ad network and ad exchange are often used together, but they do not describe exactly the same thing.

Understanding the role of each component makes it easier to understand how advertising inventory can move from a publisher to potential buyers.

LET'S DEEP DIVE

The Four Key Concepts

Simple explanations followed by an analogy to make each concept easier to understand.

01
▣

Ad Server

AD DELIVERY & MANAGEMENT

An ad server is software used to store, manage, select and deliver advertisements to websites, applications and other digital properties.

SIMPLE ANALOGY

Think of an ad server like a warehouse and delivery system. Ads are stored and managed in the warehouse, while the delivery system decides which ad should be delivered to an available ad space.

02
⇄

Ad Network

INVENTORY AGGREGATION

An ad network acts as an intermediary between advertisers and publishers. It aggregates advertising inventory from multiple publishers and makes that inventory available to advertisers.

SIMPLE ANALOGY

Imagine a company collecting advertising space from many sports websites and packaging that inventory so advertisers can reach audiences across those sites.

03
↑

SSP

SUPPLY-SIDE PLATFORM

An SSP is a technology platform that helps publishers manage, sell and optimize their advertising inventory by connecting their inventory with demand partners, ad exchanges and other sources of demand.

SIMPLE ANALOGY

An SSP works like a sales and optimization platform for publishers, helping make their available advertising inventory accessible to multiple sources of demand.

04
◇

Ad Exchange

DIGITAL MARKETPLACE

An ad exchange is a digital marketplace where advertising inventory can be bought and sold between buyers and sellers, often through automated auctions.

SIMPLE ANALOGY

Think of an ad exchange as a digital marketplace where available advertising inventory is offered to buyers and competing bids can determine which buyer gets the opportunity to serve the ad.

PROGRAMMATIC ECOSYSTEM

How These Components Can Connect

A simplified example of how advertising technology components can interact.

Publisher Makes advertising inventory available
→
Ad Server Manages and delivers advertising
→
SSP Connects publisher supply with demand
→
Ad Exchange Marketplace for buying and selling inventory
→
Buyer / DSP Participates in buying opportunities

One Important Thing to Remember

The diagram above is a simplified example and should not be interpreted as a mandatory path for every advertising impression.

Different publishers, SSPs, ad exchanges, ad servers and demand partners can use different architectures and integrations.

An SSP may connect with multiple demand sources, while an ad exchange can operate as a marketplace connecting buyers and sellers. Some technologies can also be closely integrated with one another.

QUICK COMPARISON

What's the Difference?

A simple comparison of the four concepts.

Technology Primary Role Mainly Helps
Ad Server Manage, select and deliver ads Publishers / Advertisers
Ad Network Aggregate and sell advertising inventory Publishers & Advertisers
SSP Manage, sell and optimize publisher inventory Publishers
Ad Exchange Facilitate buying and selling of inventory Buyers & Sellers
EASY WAY TO REMEMBER

Think of It This Way

Four simple descriptions to remember the roles.

Ad Server "Manage and deliver the ads."
Ad Network "Aggregate inventory and connect it with advertisers."
SSP "Help publishers sell and optimize inventory."
Ad Exchange "Marketplace where inventory can be bought and sold."
