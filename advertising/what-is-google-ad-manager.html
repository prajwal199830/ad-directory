What is Google Ad Manager?

Google Ad Manager (GAM) is a comprehensive ad management and monetization platform designed to help publishers and content creators manage, sell, and optimize their digital advertising inventory.

Publishers can use Google Ad Manager to manage advertising across a wide range of digital properties, including websites, mobile apps, video content, connected TV (CTV), and games.

The platform provides tools for managing direct and programmatic advertising, including campaign trafficking, ad serving, inventory management, forecasting, reporting, yield optimization, and programmatic demand.

Google Ad Manager can also integrate with Google Ad Exchange, allowing eligible publishers to access programmatic demand from advertisers and buyers.

Google Ad Manager Terminology

Ad Unit

An Ad Unit represents a defined piece of advertising inventory that a publisher has created in Google Ad Manager.

Examples include common display sizes such as:

  • 728 × 90
  • 300 × 250
  • 320 × 50

Line Item

A Line Item defines how an advertising campaign should be delivered.

It can contain settings such as:

  • Start date
  • End date
  • Priority or delivery settings
  • Targeting
  • Creative requirements
  • Expected impressions or clicks

Creative

A Creative is the actual advertising asset or content that is served to the user.

  • Banner
  • HTML5 ad
  • Image
  • Video
  • Native creative

Key-Value Targeting

Key-values allow publishers to pass custom targeting information with an ad request and use that information for targeting and reporting where supported.

Key: section
Value: sports
Key: user_type
Value: premium

Order

An Order groups related line items associated with an advertiser or advertising relationship.

Targeting

Targeting determines where, when, and under what conditions an eligible ad can be delivered.

  • Ad unit
  • Geography
  • Device
  • Operating system
  • Browser
  • Content
  • Key-values
  • Custom criteria

Forecasting

Forecasting helps publishers estimate available inventory and evaluate whether a campaign can potentially deliver its requested volume.

Example: An advertiser requests 1 million impressions. Forecasting can help determine whether sufficient eligible inventory may be available.

Programmatic Advertising

Programmatic advertising allows publishers to sell advertising inventory through automated buying and selling systems.

Depending on eligibility and configuration, Google Ad Manager can support programmatic demand and transaction types such as:

  • Google Ad Exchange
  • Open Auction
  • Programmatic Guaranteed
  • Preferred Deals
  • Private Auctions

Yield Management

Yield management is the process of maximizing the value generated from a publisher's available advertising inventory.

Publishers can evaluate different sources of demand and determine how eligible opportunities should compete for an impression.

Ad Request

An ad request is a request generated by a website, mobile application, video player, or another supported environment asking an advertising system for an ad.

User opens content → Ad slot becomes available → Ad Request → Ad Server → Eligible Demand → Ad Served

Important Advertising Metrics

Ad Impression

An impression is a recorded ad serving or impression event according to the applicable measurement methodology.

Fill Rate

Fill rate is commonly used to describe the proportion of ad requests that result in an ad impression.

Fill Rate = Ad Impressions ÷ Ad Requests × 100

eCPM

eCPM stands for Effective Cost Per Thousand Impressions.

It is commonly used to compare revenue performance across different advertising sources.

eCPM = Revenue ÷ Impressions × 1,000
